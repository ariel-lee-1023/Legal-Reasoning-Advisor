## Mental Model (read first)

Identify the estate, the person's route to it, the instrument or event that may change it, and the remedy available at that stage. Similar words or the same physical possession can have different legal effects because the parties stand in different relations and acquired them differently. Coke's commentary combines definitions, divisions, examples, counterexamples, instruments, statutes, customs, maxims, and reported cases; its discipline is to test their legal operation together.

This is a selective methodological distillation of the thirteen chapters of Book III. It does not provide a usable statement of contemporary land law. Citations use Littleton section numbers and Coke folios, with PDF pages where visually checked. The scan interleaves Littleton's text, Coke's commentary, and much later editorial notes. An editor's explanation or later statutory update is not Coke's statement. The file's “2 Volumes” label does not establish possession of Volume I.

## Frameworks & Structure

### 1–2. Of Parceners; Parceners by Custome — §§241 onward; 265 onward

**Coparcenary.** Littleton starts with acquisition by descent: co-heiresses can have a shared inheritance before partition. The legally important issue is how their positions arose, not merely that multiple people occupy the same ground. Identify the common source of title, the persons succeeding, and whether the relevant act divides possession or changes the character of the title.

**Custom as an operative premise.** The chapter on customary parceners uses gavelkind in Kent, with descent to multiple sons, to contrast the general account. The custom must be made part of the pleading. A conclusion drawn from the general category can fail because a legally cognizable custom changes the operative conditions. Neither frequency of conduct nor a local label proves that custom.

**Partition by agreement / partition by writ.** Section 276 allows a consensual partition in which one parcener takes separately and others remain together; Coke contrasts partition under the king's writ, where each must have a separate share. Identical practical aims do not imply identical procedural powers. Identify whether parties are acting by agreement or invoking an official remedy before importing its conditions.

**Hotchpot.** The discussion around §§266–275 concerns the treatment of a gift in frankmarriage when sharing an inheritance. Section 275 refuses to extend the adjustment to every other gift in tail. The lesson is the specificity of the legal mechanism: a superficially similar advancement is not necessarily governed by the same rule. Historical marriage and inheritance assumptions belong to the example, not the advisor's normative commitments.

### 3. Of Joyntenants — §277 onward

**Joint title and survivorship.** Joint tenancy is introduced through an acquisition under a joint grant. Compare the instrument and its recipients before concluding that shared occupation is joint tenancy. Interests, acquisition, and the later consequences of death or alienation need distinct analysis; a common physical asset does not settle them.

**Whole and share.** Coke's discussion of two joint tenants making a conditional feoffment treats each as having conveyed his own part: the fact that one enters after breach does not automatically give that person the whole. Identify the share the actor had power to affect. A form of words referring to the whole property can conceal a limit derived from the actor's legal position.

**Textual uncertainty.** At §277 Coke objects to the printed wording; the later editor records that the early editions nevertheless agree with it while considering Coke's addition necessary to the sense. A corrected reading, an original reading, and a commentator's explanation should remain distinguishable. Do not silently turn an emendation into quoted primary text.

### 4. Of Tenants in Common — §292 onward

**Several titles, common occupation.** Littleton's example has one joint tenant convey his interest to a third party. The remaining tenant and the alienee now derive their shares through different titles although their occupation remains common. Coke distinguishes acquisition by purchase, descent, or prescription. “Purchase” in this historical vocabulary is not confined to paying money.

**Reason from the transition.** Describe the before-state, conveyance, persons affected, and after-state. This explains a classification more reliably than matching appearances to a label. The same pattern helps diagnose a modern argument, but the historical consequence cannot be carried over without applicable law.

**Editorial expansion.** This chapter contains extensive later notes on customs and legal history. They help identify the edition's layers; they are not independent evidence that Coke held every historical or comparative proposition in those notes. The present reference compresses them and does not convert them into current doctrine.

### 5. Of Estates upon Condition — §325 onward; Co. Litt. 201a–b

**Condition in deed / condition in law.** Coke distinguishes conditions expressed by the parties in legally operative terms from conditions supplied by law without those words. Identify the source of the condition before treating a missing clause as decisive.

**Condition precedent / condition subsequent.** A condition may precede the legal effect or qualify an already existing estate. The second structure does not imply that the estate never existed. Ask whether the consequence concerns creation, enlargement, or defeat of an interest, and when it occurs.

**Voidable by entry or claim / void ipso facto.** Coke expressly distinguishes these consequences. In Littleton's rent example, the stipulated default allows the feoffor or heirs to enter and restore the former estate. The trigger and the divesting act are separate. Do not convert every breach into automatic termination or assume that every interested third party may act on it.

**Affirmative / negative; single / conjunctive / disjunctive.** The form of a condition changes what constitutes performance or breach. Parse who must do what, in what combination, by when, and with what alternatives. A general paraphrase can remove the very element that controls the result.

**Remedy and actor.** The later editorial note at §325 explains the historically restricted resumption remedy. Keep that note's authorship separate from Coke's divisions. For present use, independently identify who may invoke the actual remedy and whether notice, election, or other acts are required.

### 6. Discents which toll Entries — §385 onward; Co. Litt. 237a–b

**Right to enter / remedy by action.** In Littleton's example, a disseisor dies seised and the land descends to the heir. The disseisee's entry is taken away, leaving a writ of entry to recover the land. The loss of one method of recovery is not the extinction of every legally recognized claim.

**Act of law / party's act.** Coke emphasizes the heir's acquisition by descent rather than personal taking. Whether a change occurred by operation of law or by a person's act may affect available responses. Do not reduce the sequence to “A has possession, therefore A owns free of B.”

**Title / possession / remedy.** State all three. The same person may lack possession, retain a claim to recover, and be barred from immediate entry. That separation prevents an advisor from answering an ownership question solely with a procedural fact.

### 7. Of Continuall Claime — §414 onward; especially §§420–421, Co. Litt. 254a

**Preservative act.** The chapter considers acts maintaining the ability to enter despite a later descent. A claim can have a legal function apart from immediate recovery of possession. The person's ability to approach the land, the reason for not doing so, and timing can become material because the legal mechanism makes them so.

**Purpose-specific entry.** Coke at §414 distinguishes entry to make a claim from entry to take profits during a subsisting term. Permission for one act-purpose combination is not a blanket privilege to perform every use of the property. State the object of the act rather than simply saying “entry is lawful.”

**Argumentum ab authoritate est fortissimum in lege.** At §420 Coke supports Littleton's proposition with a reported assize and corrects the printed reference from plea 32 to plea 23. The general preference for reported authority is enacted through source checking. Locating a case and showing it actually supports the point are separate tasks. This passage is at PDF p. 323, visually checked during the rebuild.

**Limits of analogy.** A preservative act in this historical scheme is not a universal modern limitation-tolling device. Extract the question—what act preserves which legal route, under which authority?—and obtain present law separately.

### 8. Of Releases — §444 onward; Co. Litt. 264b, 272b

**Release of right / release of action.** The opening division distinguishes releases concerning interests in land or goods from releases of real, personal, or mixed actions. Extinguishing a particular action and conveying or surrendering the underlying position need not be identical operations. Identify precisely what the instrument releases.

**Words plus recipient's existing position.** At §465, a release by a reversioner can enlarge a tenant's estate; its scope depends on the recipient's possession, the releasor's interest, and the legally effective words. Repeating the same clause in a different relationship may produce a different result. Analyze operative effect rather than treating “release” as a fixed consequence.

**Use and trust, Co. Litt. 272b.** Coke describes the use as confidence associated with the estate and its holder, with the beneficiary's remedy in Chancery rather than at common law. Hohfeld begins his article by revisiting this description. Preserve the forum-specific statement: absence of a common-law remedy does not establish absence of an equitable relation. The source encounter here is a real tension, not a consensus that “use” has one timeless analytical meaning.

**Later commentary.** The editor's note at the opening explains releases against degrees of title and possession. It is useful context, explicitly later, not interchangeable with Coke's own text. This reference does not attempt an exhaustive modern doctrine of releases, uses, or trusts.

### 9. Of Confirmation — §515 onward; Co. Litt. 295b

**Void / voidable.** Coke defines confirmation by reference to an existing estate or right and distinguishes strengthening a voidable estate from attempting to confirm an estate void in law. The broad-sounding maxim about confirmation supplying defects is bounded immediately by this distinction. Never quote a maxim without its local qualifications.

**Existing legal substrate.** Ask what existed before the purported cure, what defect it had, whose act could cure it, and what remains afterward. A later endorsement may stabilize one defective act while being incapable of creating the missing legal basis for another.

**Express / legal operation.** The chapter differentiates forms of confirmation and investigates their effects. “Ratification” or “approval” in ordinary language does not establish that a particular curative mechanism applies; modern consequences require the actual jurisdiction's doctrine.

### 10. Of Attornement — §551 onward; Co. Litt. 309a

**Third-party assent.** Littleton's model of a grant of services requires attornment by the tenant during the grantor's life; words of agreement or a token payment may supply it. A transaction involving grantor and grantee may thus depend on a third person's legally significant act.

**Relational rationale.** Coke links the requirement to the danger of subjecting a tenant to an enemy as lord. The rationale explains the historical mechanism but does not let an advisor deduce a contemporary veto over every assignment. Separate the original institution, the condition it imposed, and later changes to the law.

### 11. Of Discontinuance — §592 onward; Co. Litt. 325a

**Route of recovery changed by alienation.** Littleton introduces discontinuance as a situation in which someone entitled to land cannot enter because of an alienation and must seek recovery through legal process. The later note expands the historical title analysis. The operative lesson is to distinguish adverse alteration of a remedy from disappearance of all substantive entitlement.

**Limits by estate and position.** At the end of the chapter, Coke qualifies a general statement about an estate tail by distinguishing seisin of freehold and inheritance from a remainder or reversion expectant on a freehold. A sentence abstracted from its subsequent limitation exaggerates the rule. Read the exception before using a category as a premise.

### 12. Of Remitter — §659 onward; Co. Litt. 347b

**Older right and later estate in one person.** Littleton describes a person coming into land through a later title but being treated as holding under an older, better one. Coke specifies an ancient remediable right meeting a later estate without folly in the holder. The legal explanation of present possession can differ from the immediate historical route by which it arrived.

**Operation of law.** Remitter is not necessarily a new grant or a choice by the holder. Distinguish automatic legal consequences from powers requiring exercise. The qualification that the older right be remediable blocks the inference that any old claim can revive automatically.

### 13. Of Warrantie — §697 onward; Co. Litt. 365a

**Lineal / collateral / commencing by disseisin.** Littleton distinguishes these warranties and their historically different barring effects. A warranty beginning in wrongful dispossession does not acquire the same effect merely because the word warranty is used. Trace its origin, descent, persons bound, statutory qualifications, and legal operation.

**Covenant real; recompense and rebutter.** Coke's account connects warranty with obligations involving land of equivalent value and with use as a bar. A substantive undertaking and its procedural deployment are related but different. Do not import present consumer or contractual meanings of warranty into this chapter.

**Communis opinio.** Coke recognizes common professional opinion and settled interpretation as sources of authority. This qualifies a claim that nothing counts until an exactly matching case exists. The author works with accumulated legal learning; the advisor must test its relevant source, scope, and weight rather than deny it categorically.

## Worked Example

Reconstruct §§385, 414–421, and 659 as a comparison of mechanisms, not one composite historical case. A person dispossessed of land may retain entry; descent to the dispossessor's heir can remove entry while leaving recovery by action. A qualifying continual claim can preserve a route which would otherwise be lost. In a different sequence, an older remediable right and a later estate can meet in one person and produce remitter by operation of law. The lawyer must therefore reconstruct events and remedies before answering “who has the right?” None of these steps follows merely from physical occupation. Read with Hohfeld, the analysis separates a privilege to enter, a power associated with entry, a continuing entitlement, and a procedural remedy; that mapping is the repository's synthesis, not Coke's vocabulary.

## Decision Rules & Judgment

- When two people possess together, trace acquisition before naming their form of co-ownership.
- When a condition is broken, determine whether its effect is automatic or requires a specified person's act.
- When an instrument supposedly changes an interest, state the parties' existing positions and the interest the actor could affect.
- When a legal remedy disappears, investigate surviving routes before concluding the entitlement is gone.
- When a later act is said to cure a defect, distinguish void from voidable and identify the legal position available to be confirmed.
- When a maxim appears conclusive, read its examples, qualifications, and later limitations.
- When an authority is pivotal, verify its locator and proposition; Coke's correction at §420 models both.
- When a passage is footnoted in this edition, establish its author and date before attributing it to Coke.

## Key Takeaways

1. Legal classification depends on title, actor, act, time, and remedy as well as words.
2. Definitions and examples work together; neither replaces source-sensitive analysis.
3. Distinguish historical legal effects from portable reasoning questions.
4. This source supplies Book III in Volume II, not the complete Institutes or both volumes.
