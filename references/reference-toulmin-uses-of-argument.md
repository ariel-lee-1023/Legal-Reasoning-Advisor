# The Uses of Argument — Stephen E. Toulmin

**Source:** Stephen E. Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument*, updated edition (Cambridge University Press, 2003; original 1958), supplied Markdown transcription. **Format**: md | **Pages**: five essays pp. 11–232; conclusion pp. 233–238 | **Sections**: 5 | **Depth**: study | **Type**: text

## Mental Model (read first)

An argument should disclose what is claimed, what is offered as grounds, and why those grounds license that claim. A warrant licenses the transition; backing establishes the warrant's authority; qualifications and rebuttal conditions state its reach. These are functions within an argument, not labels permanently attached to sentences.

The familiar layout is only part of this book. Toulmin also investigates how standards vary across fields, how modal claims work, how to distinguish warranted assertion from eventual truth, and what goes wrong when analytic arguments become the model for every substantial inference. The advisor's reconstruction and repair procedure below operationalizes those distinctions; it is not a verbatim checklist supplied by Toulmin.

## Frameworks & Structure

### I. Fields of Argument and Modals — pp. 11–40

**Phases of an argument.** Consider possible answers, test their admissibility or suitability, and assess the strength of the remaining claim. Do not jump from “not ruled out” to “established.” Different fields supply different criteria for moving between these stages.

**Force and criteria.** The force of “cannot” is to rule a possibility out; the criteria establish why it is ruled out in this field. Physical impossibility, mathematical inconsistency, and legal prohibition are not interchangeable. “The seller cannot transfer” might mean factual inability, absence of legal power, or a duty not to transfer. Identify the intended sense before assessing the modal claim; [Hohfeld](hohfeld-toolkit.md) provides the legal relations needed for this application.

**Field-invariant and field-dependent features.** The role of a challenge, a warrant, or a qualification can persist across fields while the acceptable backing and standards differ. A scientific model, an enacted rule, and a statistical generalization may all serve as backing, but one cannot substitute for another merely because their arguments share a shape. Field-dependence is not a license to accept any standard an advocate prefers.

**Diagnostic:** when an argument says “necessarily,” “probably,” “impossible,” or “must,” ask what practical force it claims and what criteria support that force. State the relevant task and standard rather than converting every modal into a probability.

### II. Probability — pp. 41–86

Toulmin examines the use of “probably” alongside “I know” and “I promise,” and distinguishes mistakes from improper assertions. A well-grounded prediction can later prove wrong. An unsupported prediction can turn out right. Evaluating an argument at the time it was made differs from checking the eventual event.

**Gardening example, pp. 53–55.** A neighbor's advice for failing gentians can be criticized because the treatment failed, because the neighbor lacked relevant expertise, or because the inquiry was deficient—for example, failing to examine relevant soil conditions. These criticisms address different things. The plants' subsequent condition does not by itself establish which initial defect existed.

**Probability and expectation / probability-relations and probabilification.** A formal relationship between propositions or frequencies is not automatically the whole practical meaning of a qualified assertion. Identify what information was available, which population or mechanism supports the estimate, and what conclusion that information warrants. Do not treat “probably” as an ornamental hedge added after an otherwise unconditional claim.

**Time-sensitive assessment:** record the grounds available at the decision time; separately record the newly learned outcome or evidence; identify whether the update reveals an earlier unjustified inference or changes an initially reasonable assessment. This prevents hindsight from masquerading as reconstruction.

**Boundary:** these philosophical analyses do not supply a current forensic probability model or a legal standard of proof. Load the evidence reference for those distinctions and verify the actual governing standard when necessary.

### III. The Layout of Arguments — pp. 87–134

#### Data, claim, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal

| Role | What it does | Question that elicits it | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data / grounds (D) | Offers facts from which the argument proceeds | What have you got to go on? | Restating the conclusion as a datum; treating testimony as its own truth |
| Claim (C) | States what the argument seeks to establish | What exactly follows? | Sliding from breach to invalidity or from some support to certainty |
| Warrant (W) | Licenses this kind of transition from D to C | Why do those grounds support that conclusion? | Inserting a universal merely to make the conclusion follow |
| Backing (B) | Supplies authority or support for W | Why accept that warrant? | More instances of D that never establish the governing bridge |
| Qualifier (Q) | States the force with which C is advanced | Necessarily, presumptively, conditionally, or with what limitation? | Keeping “must” although W supports only a defeasible step |
| Rebuttal conditions (R) | Identifies circumstances defeating this application | Under what circumstances would this warrant not carry this claim? | A generic “unless exceptions apply,” or treating an imagined exception as established |

The diagram can be rendered in prose: **D supports Q–C through W, backed by B, unless R.** A concise argument need not display all six boxes. Make the disputed function explicit and keep its support traceable.

#### Distinguishing the functions — pp. 89–100

Additional information can supply a new ground, support a warrant, or answer an exception. Its role depends on what question it answers. A condition excluding an exception is not necessarily the initial datum. One statement may be a claim in an upstream argument and a datum in the next; expand that subargument when its support matters.

Backing is not the same thing as the warrant stated more confidently. “The policy says so” needs the actual provision, scope, relevant date, and legal status for a practical legal inference. Conversely, a quotation of a provision does not by itself explain why the facts satisfy it. Separate the text from the proposed interpretation and application.

The layout does not decide who bears the procedural burden concerning each condition. A logical role is not a rule of evidence or pleading. Consult the applicable legal rule before demanding that one party disprove every imaginable exception.

#### Universal premises and formal validity — pp. 100–114

“All As are Bs” can function as a report about a checked collection or as an inference license. The compact sentence can conceal a transition between reported observations and their proposed general use. Ask how the general statement was established and which function it performs here.

**Warrant-using** arguments employ an accepted warrant; **warrant-establishing** arguments defend a new warrant or its legitimacy. Do not answer a challenge to the warrant with another instance that already assumes it. A proposal to extend liability to a new category is often doing warrant-establishing work even if dressed as routine application.

**Operational repair:** if the speaker relies on a generalization not supplied by the record, write it as a reconstructed candidate. Identify the evidence or authority that would establish it. If more than one bridge fits, show only the materially different alternatives and explain how their consequences differ.

#### Analytic and substantial arguments — pp. 114–125

For Toulmin, an analytic argument's backing already includes the information presented in its conclusion. His example about Jack's sisters and their hair color depends on having checked every sister, including the one now discussed. A substantial argument goes beyond such inclusion.

This distinction is not a synonym for legal/scientific, deductive/inductive, valid/invalid, or certain/probable. A substantial argument can be expressed in a formally valid way if an appropriate warrant is supplied; its substantive justification still requires examination. Analyticity also depends on the information involved, not just the presence of words such as “all.”

#### Crucial distinctions and the perils of simplicity — pp. 125–134

Keep separate: formal validity; warrant-using versus warrant-establishing; analytic versus substantial; the presence of quantifier words; and conclusive versus tentative force. These distinctions may coincide in favored textbook examples without doing so generally. A qualification can be needed because data are incomplete or temporally qualified even when an argument looks formally tidy.

**Failure condition:** adding “If these facts, then exactly my conclusion” guarantees a convenient shape but provides no independent warrant. A reconstruction has helped only if its bridge is open to assessment on grounds beyond the conclusion it was built to produce.

### IV. Working Logic and Idealised Logic — pp. 135–194

Toulmin criticizes the elevation of analytic syllogisms into the universal standard for substantial reasoning. The success of a formal representation in organizing a calculation is a different issue from the fit of that calculation to the problem. A model can be internally impeccable and applied under the wrong conditions.

**Use in this advisor:** test a formal inference for validity and test its factual and legal applicability separately. Do not reject formal logic in response to Toulmin's criticism, and do not attribute this book's claims about the limits of formal logic to MacCormick or Prakken. Their approaches are combined here at the level of complementary analytical operations, not declared philosophically identical.

### V. The Origins of Epistemological Theory — pp. 195–232

Toulmin relates philosophical problems about induction, intuition, and knowledge to the overextension of analytic criteria. Judging all empirical arguments “from outside time” can collapse the distinction between a soundly grounded prediction and a guarantee against future surprise.

**Use:** preserve the state of information against which an argument was assessed. If a later fact defeats an inference, identify what changed; do not pretend that the earlier argument contained that fact. Equally, prior reasonableness does not justify keeping a conclusion after its support has failed. The epistemological discussion provides this caution, not an automatic rule for deciding contested legal claims.

## Worked Example

### Source example reconstructed: Harry's nationality — pp. 93–98

Toulmin uses birthplace in Bermuda as the datum, presumed British nationality as the qualified claim, a nationality warrant as the bridge, the relevant legislation as backing, and specified parentage or later nationality changes as possible rebuttal conditions.

The instructional point is the division of functions. Birthplace evidence answers a different challenge from legislation; an exception about later naturalization can matter without disputing birthplace. The example's nationality assumptions are historical and schematic, not a rule for deciding any present person's citizenship.

**Counterexample and revision:** discovering that the presumed warrant is not in force for the relevant date attacks its backing or applicability. Learning an exceptional fact attacks this application. Neither discovery establishes a particular alternative nationality without another supported route. Do not erase the ground merely because its original inference has failed.

### Constructed legal reconstruction: an automated receipt

Argument: “The portal shows an upload at 16:58, so the appeal was filed on time.” Assume no filing rule has been supplied.

- **D, supplied:** a portal entry records an upload at 16:58. It is evidence of the entry, with authenticity and timezone still potentially relevant.
- **C, supplied:** timely legal filing.
- **W, reconstructed:** an upload of this kind, before the applicable deadline, counts as filing. The speaker has not supplied this rule.
- **B, needed:** the governing filing provision or supplied procedural rule, including which event counts and the relevant cutoff. A screenshot cannot itself establish the law.
- **Q:** conditional on authenticity, correct time interpretation, the correct filing event, and satisfaction of the rule's conditions.
- **R, possible rather than found:** a failed submission, a required subsequent acceptance event, or a rule excluding this type of upload. State only relevant possibilities supported by the context.

Now supply a fictional rule: completed submissions received before 17:00 count as filed. The entry says “draft uploaded”; acceptance occurred at 17:04. The original W fails on the supplied record. This defeats the timeliness argument; it does not by itself establish dismissal if another supplied extension or saving rule applies.

Change one fact: a verified completed-submission receipt records 16:58. The repaired argument is strong under the stipulated rule. Do not continue asking speculative questions once the material conditions have been established. If a grace-period rule is later supplied, formulate it as a separate route or qualification, rather than retrospectively describing the draft upload as completed filing.

## Decision Rules & Judgment

- If grounds are disputed, test their support. If the transition is disputed, expose the warrant. If the warrant's authority is disputed, seek backing.
- Mark each consequential premise as stated, reconstructed, assumed for analysis, or established by a supplied/verified source. A plausible reconstruction is not a quotation or admission.
- Retain the strongest plausible reading consistent with the speaker's actual claim. Label an improvement as a repair; do not silently attribute it to the speaker.
- State a concrete rebuttal condition and what would establish it. A possibility does not establish that the condition holds.
- Revise the qualifier when the warrant's strength changes. Revise or withdraw the claim when the warranted route fails, after checking other routes.
- Judge initial warrantability and subsequent truth separately. Avoid both hindsight condemnation and refusal to update.

## Key Takeaways

The essential product is an assessable bridge, with the right backing, force, and exceptions. Toulmin's layout locates questions; it does not answer them automatically. Preserve functional roles, time of assessment, field-specific standards, and the difference between faithful reconstruction and proposed repair.
