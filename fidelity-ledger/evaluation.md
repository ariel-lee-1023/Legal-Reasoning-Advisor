## 1. Permission is not protection

**Prompt:** “The owner gave me permission to eat the salad. Therefore the owner owes me a duty not to hold the dish away.”

**Sample response:** Permission can establish your privilege to eat without establishing a claim that the owner refrain from interference. Identify an independent promise or applicable rule supplying that duty. The permission alone does not prove your conclusion, but it also does not prove that every form of interference is lawful.

**Review:** Satisfies the criterion: privilege/no-right separated from claim/duty; failure of inference not confused with proof of its opposite. Core + Hohfeld.

## 2. Duty and privilege of the same act

**Prompt:** “A owes B a duty to enter A's land. Does that make entry unprivileged as against B?”

**Sample response:** No. A privilege to enter is the absence of a duty not to enter. It can coexist with a duty to enter. The opposite duty has contrary content.

**Review:** Satisfies the content-sensitive opposites criterion. Hohfeld's land example, pp. 32–33.

## 3. Effective but wrongful transfer

**Prompt:** “A validly promises B not to transfer to C. Stipulate that A nevertheless retains a legal power to transfer and makes a qualifying transfer. Did C acquire nothing?”

**Sample response:** That conclusion contradicts the stipulated transfer power and its effective exercise. A can breach the duty to B while effectively changing C's position. B's remedy for breach and any distinct invalidity rule require separate analysis.

**Review:** Satisfies the power/privilege distinction and avoids inventing a remedy. Core + Hohfeld.

## 4. Trigger is not exercise

**Prompt:** “The deed says default permits the grantor to terminate by a specified act. Default occurred, but that act has not occurred. Has title automatically ended?”

**Sample response:** Not on those premises. Default enables the specified power; its exercise is a separate condition of the legal change. A different rule of automatic termination would need its own basis.

**Review:** Satisfies the sequence criterion. Core + Institutes + Hohfeld.

## 5. Loss of entry is not loss of every entitlement

**Prompt:** “In Littleton's descent example, the claimant can no longer enter. Has every means of recovering the land disappeared?”

**Sample response:** No. In §385 the loss of entry leaves recovery by a writ of entry. That is a historical distinction between routes of recovery, not a current limitation rule.

**Review:** Satisfies remedial and historical-boundary criteria. Institutes.

## 6. The later document is evidence

**Prompt:** “Here is the paper now. The stipulated rule requires delivery before the deadline, and delivery is disputed. Is timely exercise proved?”

**Sample response:** Present inspection supports the existence and content of the paper; it does not by itself establish earlier timely delivery. The legal issue is the operative delivery; the evidential issue is how to prove it. The allocation of proof depends on the applicable procedure.

**Review:** Satisfies operative/evidential, timing, and burden-boundary criteria. Hohfeld + taxonomy.

## 7. A new device under an enacted definition

**Prompt:** “The applicable Act expressly includes all powered devices carrying a person. The device meets that description and no exception applies. No court has considered this model.”

**Sample response:** On those premises, the definition includes it. Absence of a case about this model does not itself create a legal gap. A challenge would need to address the definition's application, a relevant exception, or the facts rather than novelty alone.

**Review:** Satisfies the correction to the old anti-definition stance. Core + taxonomy.

## 8. Independent grounds

**Prompt:** “A court dismisses on two independent grounds. I have defeated one. Must the dismissal be reversed?”

**Sample response:** Not merely for that reason. The other sufficient ground remains. Identify its support and the applicable review rules. Even removal of both grounds may mean further proceedings rather than judgment on the ultimate claim.

**Review:** Satisfies alternative-route and remedy criteria. Core + taxonomy + digest.

## 9. A superior precedent considered unjust

**Prompt:** “Stipulate a binding superior-court rule and no recognized exception for this trial court. Can it disregard the rule because Duxbury says precedent is defeasible?”

**Sample response:** No. The philosophical account does not supply a power the stipulated institutional rules deny. The objection may support an authorized appeal or reform argument while the trial court remains constrained.

**Review:** Satisfies the competence boundary without denying jurisprudential defeasibility. Core + Duxbury.

## 10. A distinction that contradicts the earlier result

**Prompt:** “The precedent grants X on A, B, C. It also lacked E. Can I preserve that precedent by saying X requires E?”

**Sample response:** That proposed condition would defeat the earlier outcome as well. It therefore needs more than the label distinguishing: explain whether another ground preserves the result or whether you are proposing repudiation and have authority to do so.

**Review:** Satisfies the counterfactual test while preserving possible alternative grounds. Duxbury.

## 11. Fragmented opinions

**Prompt:** “Three judges agree on the order but give incompatible grounds. The shortest concurrence has the narrowest facts. Extract the binding rule without a jurisdiction or aggregation rule.”

**Sample response:** I can state the order and map the propositions each judge supports. Shortness or factual narrowness does not establish the controlling rule. Without a governing aggregation rule or a defensible common proposition, a single binding ratio is not established from this description.

**Review:** Satisfies opinion-alignment and missing-authority criteria. Core + digest + Duxbury.

## 12. Alternative rationes and weighty dicta

**Prompt:** “If deleting each of two sufficient grounds leaves the result unchanged, are both dicta? And can all other dicta be discarded?”

**Sample response:** A mechanical deletion test cannot resolve independent sufficient grounds. Record both and apply the system's approach to their authority. Other considered dicta may carry persuasive weight even if outside the holding; classify and assess them separately.

**Review:** Satisfies the nonmechanical-ratio criterion. Duxbury + digest.

## 13. Coke and deductive reasoning

**Prompt:** “Artificial reason proves Coke rejected all natural-law premises and syllogisms, correct?”

**Sample response:** No. Calvin's Case expressly uses both. The better reading is that legal judgment requires learned engagement with legal sources and institutions, not that Coke forbids every general principle or deductive form.

**Review:** Satisfies the source-counterexample criterion. Selected Writings, printed pp. 224–225.

## 14. Public benefit and authority

**Prompt:** “A board has a power to repair drains; it wants to acquire land for a new river. Assume the project is beneficial. Is authority established?”

**Sample response:** Benefit alone does not establish that the repair power includes acquisition and new construction. Examine the authorizing text, exceptions, actors, and any separate procedure. Coke's Isle of Ely illustrates that distinction but does not determine present board powers.

**Review:** Satisfies the actor/act/authority and historical-boundary criteria. Coke Reports + taxonomy.

## 15. Statutory prohibition and imprisonment

**Prompt:** “An Act prohibits unlicensed practice in one clause and authorizes imprisonment for deficient practice in another. Does the first violation alone establish imprisonment power under the second?”

**Sample response:** No. Read the distinct triggers, actors, and sanctions together. The prohibition alone does not establish that the imprisonment clause applies. Bonham's report uses this kind of clause-specific analysis; a broad constitutional theory is not required to identify the missing premise.

**Review:** Satisfies the whole-text and remedial-power criteria. Coke Reports + Hohfeld.

## 17. Closed-record and current-law boundaries

**Prompt:** “With no outside research, use this 2008 discussion to certify that an illustrative case remains binding today.”

**Sample response:** The supplied discussion can establish how Duxbury used the case in 2008, not its current force. I can assess the argument within that record; later treatment and the present forum's rules remain unverified.

**Review:** Satisfies the distinction between source-derived explanation and current authority. Core + digest.

## 18. Ordinary request, scale, and language

**Prompt:** “用两句话解释，为什么‘有权转让’与‘转让不违约’不是一回事。”

**Sample response:** “有权转让”可能是说转让能有效改变法律关系；“转让不违约”说的则是实施这一行为是否违反对他人的义务。一个人可能仍能作出有效转让，同时因违反不得转让的约定承担相应责任，具体后果还要看适用规则。

**Review:** Satisfies concise user-language explanation without a full digest or an invented jurisdiction-specific remedy. Core + Hohfeld.
